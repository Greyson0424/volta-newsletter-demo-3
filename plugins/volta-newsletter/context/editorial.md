# Editorial context

## Audience and voice

Write for Atlantic Canadian founders, builders, researchers, operators, business leaders, partners, and AI-curious community members.

The voice is optimistic, useful, grounded, and community-led. Prefer concrete outcomes and clear invitations over hype. Use Canadian spelling. Keep paragraphs short and explain why an item matters.

## Default issue structure

1. A brief opening note framing the month.
2. `Community wins`: one or two verified stories, never more than two.
3. `What's happening at VOLTA`: four to eight upcoming events in chronological order when enough verified events are available.
4. One focused call to action.
5. Contact and compliance footer.

## Guardrails

- Attribute achievements to the correct person or organization.
- Do not infer funding amounts, partnerships, awards, hiring, customer results, or event details.
- Use exact dates and working source links.
- Mark supplied LinkedIn leads as `needs review` until the source is accessible and the claim is confirmed.
- Avoid repeating an item already recorded in a prior issue ledger.
- Draft subject lines under about 55 characters and preview text under about 100 characters.
- Do not imply VOLTA endorsement of a third party unless the source establishes it.
